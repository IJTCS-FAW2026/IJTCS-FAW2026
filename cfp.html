Call for Papers

Founded in 2007, the Conference on Frontiers of Algorithmic Wisdom (FAW) provides an international forum for advances in algorithms, optimization, and theoretical computer science. IJTCS-FAW 2026 celebrates the 20th edition of FAW, marking two decades of research and scholarly exchange. The conference is currently part of the CCF High-Quality International Academic Conference Incubation Program.

IJTCS-FAW 2026 will take place November 7–10, 2026, in Beijing, China. The program will feature invited talks, technical paper presentations, and academic forums across algorithms, blockchain, computational economics, and algorithmic game theory.

Authors are invited to submit original and unpublished research papers through OpenReview. All submissions will be peer-reviewed and evaluated for originality, technical soundness, significance, and overall contribution. Submissions must not be under simultaneous review by another conference or journal.

Tracks

Papers will be reviewed within four tracks. Typical but not exclusive topics of interest are listed below.

Track A

Frontiers of Algorithmic Wisdom

This track focuses on current and emerging research in algorithms, discrete structures, optimization, complexity, and learning-augmented methods. Typical but not exclusive topics of interest are:

  • Algorithms and data structures
  • Discrete and combinatorial structures
  • Approximation and online algorithms
  • Combinatorial optimization
  • Graph algorithms
  • Computational geometry
  • Complexity theory
  • Parameterized algorithms
  • Learning-augmented algorithms

Track B

Blockchain Theory and Technology

This track focuses on theoretical and technological advances in blockchain, distributed ledgers, cryptoeconomic systems, and decentralized markets. Typical but not exclusive topics of interest are:

  • Consensus and scalability
  • Smart contracts
  • Distributed ledgers
  • Security and privacy
  • Interoperability and cross-chain protocols
  • Decentralized finance
  • Digital currencies
  • Automated market makers
  • Tokenomics
  • Blockchain mechanism design
  • Cryptoeconomic systems

Track C

Computational Economics and Algorithmic Game Theory

This track focuses on computational questions in economics, markets, mechanism design, game theory, learning, and AI-driven platforms. Typical but not exclusive topics of interest are:

  • Equilibrium computation and efficiency
  • Mechanism and market design
  • Auctions and pricing
  • Matching markets
  • Fair division and social choice
  • Information and contract design
  • Learning in games and markets
  • Platform economics
  • Computational advertising
  • The economics of AI

Track D

Foundations of Artificial Intelligence

This track focuses on theoretical foundations of learning and intelligent systems, including reliable, agentic, generative, and economically grounded AI. Typical but not exclusive topics of interest are:

  • Learning Theory and Algorithms
  • Online Learning and Bandits
  • Reinforcement Learning and Control
  • Optimization for AI
  • Theory of Deep Learning
  • Foundation Models and Large Language Models
  • Generative Models and Sampling
  • Causal Learning and Inference
  • Agentic AI and Multi-Agent Systems
  • Formal Methods and Neuro-Symbolic AI
  • Robust and Reliable AI
  • Interpretability, Fairness, and Privacy
  • AI Safety and Alignment
  • Data Valuation, Pricing, and Markets
  • Economics of AI Models and Computation
  • AI Platforms and Model Ecosystems
